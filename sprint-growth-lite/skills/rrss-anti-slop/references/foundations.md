# Foundations: anti-slop

Full citations are in `biblioteca/referencias.md`.

**Ogilvy (1983), *Ogilvy on Advertising*.** Specific facts and plain language persuade better than generalities in his experience. *Used here:* the specificity and proof checks.

**Heath & Heath (2007), *Made to Stick*.** Concrete and credible beats abstract and generic. *Used here:* the rewrite examples.

**Orwell (1946), "Politics and the English Language", *Horizon*.** Stale phrases and vague words let writers avoid thinking. *Used here:* the cliché list and the habit of reading aloud. *Limit:* an essay on style, not an experiment.

**Google Search Central, "Creating helpful, reliable, people-first content".** Content made mainly to attract clicks, without original value, is discouraged. *Used here:* rule 1 and the proof check; also relevant to captions that feed web pages.

**Method note.** The linter is a pattern matcher with an editable list in `scripts/rrss_lint.py`. It catches habits, not meaning. Extend the list with the brand's own banned phrases.
