# Paid media: the yardstick of each campaign

A campaign is measured by what the platform was asked to do, not by what we would like it to do. Judging with the wrong yardstick is worse than having no opinion, because the error looks as confident as a hit. Reply in the user's language (Chilean Spanish, never voseo, when the user writes in Spanish).

## When to use it

- Before issuing any campaign verdict (`pauta-diagnostico`, step 1).
- When someone asks for a single number for the whole account.
- When comparing native-form leads with leads arriving through the website.

## Process

1. Identify the campaign's **configured objective** (not the one the client believes it has).
2. Look up the yardstick table in `references/objective-yardsticks.md`: own KPI, alarm signal and what NOT to conclude.
3. If the campaign is lead-based, apply the hard native-versus-web lead rule (same reference).
4. Check the bid strategy: the minimum volume needed to judge depends on it.
5. Deliver **two levels**: fine judgment per campaign with its yardstick, and a big picture by stage without mixing KPIs.

## Rules

1. Traffic is judged by cost per quality visit, video by cost per sustained view, reach by cost per person reached. Never by purchases.
2. KPIs of different nature are never averaged or added.
3. Without a goal declared by the client, the only possible judgment is against the previous period, and that is said.
4. External benchmarks are indicative; recalibrate with the account's own data.

## Method signature

- Two campaigns share a bucket only if they share the yardstick.
- If an objective has no measurable yardstick, it gets no verdict. A judgment with the wrong ruler is worse than none.
- The yardstick is one number; the diagnosis is everything that explains it.
