# Foundations: the yardstick by objective

Full citations are in `biblioteca/referencias.md`.

**Lewis & Rao (2015), *Quarterly Journal of Economics* 130(4).** Across 25 field experiments, the median confidence interval of ROI exceeds 100 percentage points. Practical consequence: a small campaign measured in a short window does not support a fine verdict. That is why the skill separates "fine judgment" from "big picture" and demands minimum volume.

**Gordon et al. (2019), *Marketing Science* 38(2).** Observational methods (the usual ones in the industry) often fail to recover the causal effect. Practical consequence: a retargeting campaign with high ROAS may be collecting sales that would have happened anyway; the right yardstick for it includes incrementality.

**Binet & Field (2013), IPA.** Brand-building and activation advertising operate on different horizons. Practical consequence: a reach campaign is not punished for not selling in 7 days, and a sales campaign is not applauded for cheap reach.

**Meta help center (learning phase, ~50 events in 7 days).** The bid strategy and optimization event set how much volume is needed before judging.

**General limit.** The skill's yardsticks are working criteria, not laws. Where the client declares a goal, that goal rules.
